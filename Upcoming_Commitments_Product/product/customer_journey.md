# Customer Journey

## Current-state hypothesis
1. Customer receives salary.
2. Individual card payments and direct debits accumulate in transaction history.
3. Customer mentally estimates what is still due or checks multiple merchants/apps.
4. A forgotten bill or subscription reduces the balance unexpectedly.
5. Customer reacts after the payment rather than planning before it.

This is a **design hypothesis**, not a finding from completed primary research.

## Proposed product journey
1. Customer opens Upcoming Commitments.
2. Product shows current balance, expected recurring commitments and projected available balance.
3. Customer reviews upcoming payments ordered by date.
4. Customer drills into a recurring payment and sees recent history plus predicted next amount/date.
5. Customer confirms or rejects the classification.
6. Customer sets a low-balance warning threshold.

## Next validation step
Conduct 5-8 structured user interviews and task-based usability sessions before making any claim that the journey solves a real customer problem or improves customer outcomes.
